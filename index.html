Arthroscopy (Keyhole Joint Surgery)

What is Arthroscopy?

Arthroscopy is an advanced, minimally invasive surgical procedure used to visualize, diagnose, and treat conditions inside a joint using an arthroscope—a slender, high-definition camera system. Instead of large open incisions, surgical repairs are performed through miniature keyhole punctures, significantly reducing trauma to surrounding healthy muscles and tissues.

Commonly Treated Joints & Conditions

  • Knee Arthroscopy: Arthroscopic anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) and posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) reconstructions, meniscus repairs or trims, loose body removal, and cartilage defect management.
  • Shoulder Arthroscopy: Rotator cuff repairs, Bankart repairs for recurrent dislocations, labral tear fixes, subacromial decompression for impingement, and frozen shoulder release.
  • Ankle & Other Joints: Synovitis management, cartilage debridement, and treatment of persistent joint impingement or stiffness.

Key Advantages of Arthroscopic Surgery

  • Minimal Invasiveness: Conducted through tiny incisions rather than extensive surgical cuts.
  • Reduced Pain & Blood Loss: Preserves structural soft tissues and muscle integrity during repair.
  • Faster Recovery & Mobilization: Patients experience shorter hospital stays and a quicker return to routine activities.
  • Minimal Scarring: Tiny puncture marks lead to clean cosmetic healing with minimal scar tissue formation.

Sports Medicine & Sports Injuries

What is Sports Medicine?

Sports Medicine is a specialized discipline of orthopaedics dedicated to the prevention, early diagnosis, targeted treatment, and structural rehabilitation of injuries sustained during sports, fitness routines, and high-impact physical activities. The primary objective is not merely pain relief, but restoring joint biomechanics, muscular strength, agility, and functional confidence.

Common Sports Injuries Managed

  • Ligamentous Tears: Acute ACL, PCL, and collateral ligament ruptures resulting from sudden pivoting, decelerations, or contact impact.
  • Meniscus Injuries: Fibrocartilage tears caused by forceful twisting or heavy loaded rotation of the knee joint.
  • Recurrent Shoulder Instability: Subluxations and complete dislocations resulting from overhead athletic moves or direct falls.
  • Tendon & Muscle Tears: Hamstring strains, quad tendon ruptures, rotator cuff tendinopathy, and Achilles tendon damage.

Comprehensive Treatment & Recovery Protocol

  • Accurate Clinical Diagnosis: In-depth orthopedic assessment combined with advanced imaging to distinguish non-surgical strains from surgical tears.
  • Minimally Invasive Interventions: Anatomical tissue preservation and arthroscopic reconstructions when surgical stability is required.
  • Targeted Physiotherapy Guidance: Progressive rehabilitation focused on core stability, joint range of motion, and eccentric muscle strengthening.
  • Structured Return-to-Play Pathways: Milestone-based athletic criteria to ensure the patient safely returns to full activity without the risk of re-injury.
